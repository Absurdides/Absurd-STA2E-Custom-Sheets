.: THE CORE MECHANIC (PAGE 8)

When your character attempts a task, the gamemaster tells you the Difficulty of the task (a number, usually from 0–5). Check your character sheet and select an attribute and department best fitting the desired action. Add the selected ratings together to establish your target number.

Then, roll 2d20 (sometimes more, depending upon the situation) and check if any die rolls equal to or under the target number. If a die rolls lower, it counts as a success against the task's Difficulty. The more difficult the task, the more successes are needed.

Roll too high on the dice and you fail to accomplish the task. It may put your character in danger, or worse, but it makes the story more exciting. Rolling a 20 creates a complication (page 258).

.: PERSONAL WEAPON QUALITIES (PAGES 241-242)
  • ACCURATE: If you perform the Aim minor action before making an attack with this weapon, you may re-roll up to two d20s in your dice pool, rather than only one.
  • AREA: When you succeed at an attack, additional targets in the same zone may be hit by spending 1 Momentum for each additional target (Repeatable). This attack may Succeed at Cost.

  • INTENSE: When making an attack with this weapon, you may increase the severity by spending 1 Momentum, rather than 2 (Repeatable).
  • PIERCING: A successful attack with this weapon ignores the target's Protection rating.
.: RECOVERING STRESS (PAGES 290-293)

A character can recover Stress in several ways. Each time you recover 1 Stress, clear one marked space on your Stress track. If you're Fatigued, you can only recover from Stress by resting, or if an ally helps you. Ways to recover Stress include:

■ MOMENTUM: After a successful task, you may spend 2 Momentum to remove 1 Stress (Repeatable) from yourself or one ally who can hear you. A character cannot remove more than 3 Stress in this way on any single task.

■ REST: You can recover from Stress outside of conflict by spending time resting and relaxing:

▢ A breather is the most basic form of rest. This takes only a few minutes, during which you stop all strenuous activity. You remove 4 Stress when you take a breather.
▢ A break is a more significant rest, taking at least half an hour, and up to a couple of hours. This may involve a modest meal, or taking some personal time for recreation. You remove 8 Stress when you take a break.
▢ Sleep or a similar extended rest takes several hours, typically somewhere comfortable and quiet, and is normally accompanied by a meal and/or something to drink before or after sleep. You remove all your Stress, up to your normal maximum, when you sleep.
.: RECOVERY AND HEALING (PAGES 292-293)

The First Aid action allows you to attempt a Daring + Medicine task with a Difficulty of 2 to tend to another character within Reach. If you complete the task, the patient is no longer Defeated. Alternatively, you may attempt a Daring + Medicine task with a Difficulty equal to the Injury's severity to treat an Injury the patient has suffered.

A treated Injury no longer imposes any penalty, but it is still an Injury, and it will need proper medical treatment to remove entirely.

Longer-term healing cannot be done during combat. An Injured character requires medical treatment to remove the Injury completely. This treatment is normally a Control + Medicine task with a Difficulty of X and takes X hours, where X is the severity in both cases. Add 1 to the Difficulty and the complication range if the Injury was not treated.

If the patient has multiple Injuries, treating them all at once could be treated as a challenge, or even as an extended task (with a progress track of 3x the number of Injuries), at the gamemaster's discretion.

▷ TALKING IS A FREE ACTION (PAGE 298)

Characters in the same physical space within the ship during starship combat can communicate freely: it doesn't take an action to talk unless the talking is part of another task. Most talking a character does should take place on their turn, but characters are free to comment on or interject during another character's turn.

Characters in different parts of the ship—say, the captain on the bridge and the chief engineer in main engineering—can communicate freely under normal circumstances, but damage to the ship's communications system may result in parts of the ship being cut off from communicating with one another.

▷ COLLISION DAMAGE (PAGE 309)

There are times when a ship may collide with another. This may happen accidentally—such as when a ship is attempting to dock or land and suffers a complication—or deliberately, by ramming. When one ship collides with another, each ship takes the other ship's collision damage.

A ship's collision damage rating is equal to the ship's Scale, plus half the number of zones moved before the collision. This has the Devastating and Piercing qualities. In addition, if a ship is ramming, then the ramming vessel gains the Intense quality as well.

.: MINOR ACTIONS (PAGE 298)

As described on page 288, minor actions are activities a character can undertake that don't count as a task, and which don't require dice to be rolled. You can take 1 minor action on each of your turns, and may take an additional minor action per turn by spending 1 Momentum (Immediate).

The most common starship minor actions below are specific to starship operations during combat; a character can perform personal combat minor actions (page 288) if necessary, such as when being boarded.

.: MAJOR ACTIONS (PAGE 298)

A character can attempt a single major action (typically to attempt a task) during each turn, though there are two ways a character could attempt a second major action in a turn:

■ LEADERSHIP: The Direct major action allows a character to issue an order to a subordinate, granting them an extra major action.

■ MOMENTUM: A character may spend 2 Momentum after a successful task to attempt a second major action; however, any task attempted as part of this second major action increases in Difficulty by 1.

Regardless of the method used, a character cannot attempt more than two major actions in a round. The major actions apply to operating a starship in combat. Personal conflict tasks can also be attempted where relevant, such as when repelling boarders.

.: COMMAND STATION

Central to any bridge is the Command station, often a captain's chair. More than merely a centralized seat from which to oversee the bridge crew, most captain's chairs are equipped with a limited set of controls, configured to the captain's needs. These controls often tie into internal communications, navigation, or some other function to which the captain desires quick access or additional oversight.

.: COMMUNICATIONS

While communications functions can be accessed from several different stations, many ships have a dedicated comms station; this is especially the case with ships in the 22nd and 23rd centuries. This station controls all internal and external communications systems. The communications officer is responsible for maintaining and updating the universal translator, monitoring nearby transmissions (including distress calls and signals from hostiles), handling secure or encrypted communications, and coordinating crew activities such as security responses and damage control teams.

These actions are usually assisted by the ship's Communications + Engineering. The specialized nature of the controls at this station are designed to enable complex tasks, so any Communications-related tasks performed at this station reduce their Difficulty by 1.

.: TRANSPORT

A transporter has an effective range of several thousand kilometers, and a standard transporter room can transport up to six people at once. Transporting from a transporter pad to a destination transporter pad is a Control + Engineering task with a Difficulty of 0, assisted by the ship's Sensors + Science. Transporting from a transporter pad to another site (one without a transporter pad), or vice versa, increases the Difficulty by 1, while a site-to-site transport increases the Difficulty by 2.

Deflector shields block transporter use—you cannot beam a target up or down when your own shields are active, and you cannot beam a target to or from a target whose shields are active.

.: TACTICAL

A character at tactical operates the ship's weapons and defensive systems. A ship may have a second tactical position as a separate station if it has a Security rating of 4+.

The tactical console (page 188) operates the ship's offensive and defensive systems: the weapons and shields, as well as related systems such as tractor beams. Many tactical stations allow the officer to monitor and coordinate the ship's internal security, including operation of communications—information security, handling of encrypted signals, and intercepting enemy transmissions—as well as coordinating security teams, internal doors, and forcefields. These actions are usually assisted by the ship's Weapons + Security.

▷ BREACHES CARD | 01

As an option, use these Breaches tables to generate thematic descriptions and traits for breaches affecting damaged ship systems. Use the Nature of Breach table to determine the nature of the breach inflicted, then use the other tables for the different systems to determine which subsystem is affected, and a suggested game effect.

.: NATURE OF BREACH
D20 NATURE OF BREACH
1–4 Damaged: The subsystem is mostly functional, but does occasionally pose problems. The gamemaster is encouraged to spend Threat to cause complications related to the subsystem.
5–8 Malfunctioning: The subsystem is no longer fully functional. Each turn, one character must perform a Restore minor action for that system to allow tasks to be attempted with that subsystem that turn.
9–12 Primary Offline, Switching to Backup: The subsystem has been rendered inoperable, but backup or secondary systems have taken over. Add 1 to the Difficulty of tasks using the subsystem.
13–16 Failing: The subsystem is losing power and function rapidly, and while it is still functioning now, that may change soon. You add 1 to the Difficulty of tasks relating to this subsystem, and the gamemaster may spend 1 Threat subsequently to have the subsystem fail entirely.
17–20 Offline: The subsystem has shut down or been rendered inoperable, and the backups have failed as well. You may not attempt tasks related to this subsystem.
.: COMMUNICATIONS BREACHES
D20 COMMUNICATIONS BREACH
1–7 Subspace Transceiver Array: This is a vital component of faster-than-light and long-ranged communications. Damage affects the ship's ability to call for assistance.
8–14 Internal Communications: The internal communications network of the ship has been affected, limiting the ability of the crew to contact one another.
15–20 Short-Range Communications: The ship's short-range communications—conventional radio, tight beam laser, and other electromagnetic signals—have been affected, limiting communication with other nearby vessels and stations.
.: COMPUTERS BREACHES
D20 COMPUTERS BREACHES
1–7 Main Computer: The ship's main computer system has been affected, preventing access to library information as well as most automation and control subroutines, leaving individual consoles to function alone. This may cause wider problems controlling the ship.
8–14 Holodeck: The ship's holodeck (or comparable simulator—reroll if no such equivalent exists) is malfunctioning, and may behave unpredictably.
15–20 ODN Network: The Optical Data Network has been affected, limiting the ability of data to move around the ship. This may render control consoles unreliable or inoperable as they can no longer connect to the systems they control.
.: ENGINES BREACHES
D20 ENGINE BREACHES
1–4 Impulse Engines: The ship's sublight propulsion system has been affected, impacting the ship's ability to move at sublight speeds.
5–8 Reaction Control Thrusters: The ship's maneuvering thrusters have been affected, limiting the ship's ability to make fine adjustments to its position and maneuver around obstacles.
9–12 EPS Grid: The ship's Electro-Plasma System, which feeds power to systems across the ship, has been damaged. Damaged EPS conduits often result in power failures to key systems, fires, injuries to personnel, or all three.
13–16 Warp Nacelles: One of the ship's nacelles, which allow the ship to fly at warp speeds, has been damaged. These are seldom inhabited, but damage can result in fires, plasma leaks, or dangerous radiation leaks.
17–20 Warp Core: The main reactor that powers the warp engines—and the rest of the ship—has been damaged. This can be extremely dangerous, as the ship is suddenly left without main power, and a containment failure may result in major casualties, massive damage, or even the destruction of the ship. On most ships, this will be a matter/antimatter reactor, though Romulan vessels tend to use a contained singularity drive.

.: STRUCTURE BREACHES
D20 STRUCTURE BREACHES
1–4 Inertial Dampers: An essential starship system which absorbs and counters inertial forces upon the ship; if damaged, this can make acceleration dangerous or even deadly, limiting how fast the ship can move safely.
5–7 Structural Integrity: A system of forcefields that run through the ship's superstructure, reinforcing it against the stresses of spaceflight. If damaged, the ship may suffer even greater damage as the hull collapses. A possible effect of this may be to reduce or even remove the ship's Resistance.
8–11 Hull Breach: A section of the ship's hull has been pierced, exposing the interior to hard vacuum, and potentially resulting in crew casualties. Normally, doors will seal and forcefields will be erected to isolate breaches like this, but severe damage can compromise these systems.
12–14 Life Support: The ship's atmospheric and artificial gravity systems have been affected. Short-term, the crew can survive with whatever breathable air remains in the ship, but the longer life support is damaged, the worse the problem will get.
15–17 Deflector Shields: The ship's shield generators are affected, compromising the ship's main defenses against attack.
18–20 Launch Bay: The ship's shuttle bay or auxiliary craft bay has been affected, making it difficult to launch or receive small craft.
.: SENSORS BREACHES
D20 SENSORS BREACH
1–4 Long-Range Sensor Array: Sensor arrays used to detect warp signatures and stellar phenomena several light-years away are affected.
5–8 Short-Range Sensor Array: Sensor arrays used to gain detailed information within a few hundred thousand kilometers are affected.
9–12 Transporters: The transporter systems, which rely heavily on specialized sensors, are affected.
13–16 Astrogation Sensors: A ship relies on a host of specialized navigation sensors for plotting courses and mapping hazards. Damage to these sensors can make maneuvering much riskier.
17–20 Targeting Sensors: The ship's tactical sensor arrays have been affected, potentially hindering the accuracy of attacks.
.: WEAPONS BREACHES
D20 WEAPONS BREACHES
1–8 Energy Weapons: The ship's energy weapons have been affected, limiting their ability to fire.
9–16 Torpedoes: The ship's torpedo launchers have been affected. This could be particularly dangerous if the ship's magazine—where the torpedoes are stored—is affected.
17–20 Tractor Beam Emitter: The ship's tractor beam emitters have been affected.
▷ BREACHES AND POTENT TRAITS (PAGE 310)

Multiple identical traits can be combined to create a Potent trait, with a Potency rating noted after the trait's name. The rating is equal to the number of traits combined in this way—three traits representing dense smoke would be combined into Dense Smoke 3, for example.

As breaches are a form of trait, this can be a useful way to handle especially severe damage inflicted, such as if a single attack inflicts multiple breaches.

.: BREACHES (PAGES 310-311)

A breach is a trait representing serious damage to the ship and/or crew, and is associated with the system of the ship struck by the attack or hazard. As a negative trait, its usual effect is to increase the Difficulty of tasks attempted or to make some tasks impossible.

.: REPAIRING DAMAGE

If a ship has suffered breaches, characters can attempt tasks to try and repair the damage (see page 311).

.: SYSTEMS DESTROYED

If a system suffers a number of breaches equal to or greater than half the ship's Scale, the system is destroyed. When a system is destroyed, any task which would be assisted by that system automatically fails.

If your ship suffers a number of breaches greater than the ship's Scale, the ship suffers critical damage and can no longer function. No further actions can be taken if the ship has taken critical damage. If the ship takes any additional breaches, it is destroyed.
